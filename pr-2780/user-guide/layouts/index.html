Layouts and Reports¶

Layouts¶

Home › New Layout adds a page layout (Letter, Legal, Tabloid or A5 to A0, landscape or portrait) with a map frame, title, legend, north arrow, scale bar and a dynamic credits line. The command layout.new also accepts custom width and height in millimetres and "template": "blank". Both layout.new and each layout in info include an elements array with each element's element ID, name and kind, ready to use in layout.update or layout.removeElement. The existing layout index, name and info map-frame summaries keep their meanings. Layouts open as tabs next to the maps; while one is open, its groups lead the Publish tab:

  • Publication layouts: Home › New Layout › Publication Layout builds a finished sheet or figure of the map view in one step, with layout.publication behind it:
  • Sheets (on every page size New Layout lists: Letter, Legal, Tabloid and A5 to A0, portrait or landscape; the menu lists the sizes, each with its styles under Portrait and under Landscape): a frame and margin, the title and subtitle, the map, and a footer of two boxed columns (sources, and the cartographic reference; one with nothing in it is left out, the other taking the width) with a note and the authorship under them. When the legend sits at the right of the map (its right edge in the frame's right tenth), starting between 45 % and 70 % of the frame's width, the right column starts where the legend does, unless its texts would then need a taller box. Text sizes follow the sheet's short side: an A3's are 1.41 times an A4's, an A0's four times. So do the legend's text size, the scale bar's and the graticule's (9, 8 and 8 points on A4) and the strips the graticule's labels take inside the frame. Given its size alone ("a4", the default, or any other: "letter", "a0"…), a sheet is turned to portrait or landscape, whichever the map comes out larger on: the area's extent east to west against north to south, on the map's projection, with the room the style leaves the map. The result names the profile chosen ("profile": "a4-landscape"); give it with its orientation ("a4-portrait") to have the other.
  • Figures for a journal (90, 140 or 190 mm wide, as tall as the map's shape asks) or a thesis (160 × 225 or 160 × 110 mm): the map edge to edge with a line of its projection and datum under it, at 7 pt. A figure's title and sources belong in its caption, so they are left out, and the result says so.
  • The map: the extent given (or the view) fitted north-up, without tilt, at a round scale, two significant figures, rounded up so it still fits within the strips the graticule's labels take inside the frame. A graticule's step follows the extent (2° for a state, 15′ for a city), with labels on every edge of a sheet. The north arrow, the legend (sized to what it lists), the scale bar with the numeric scale under it (in a box the size of the bar) and the locator sit in the frame's corners, clear of the graticule's labels. The arrangement says which corner is each one's. classic (the default favourite) puts the locator at the top left, the arrow at the top right, the legend at the bottom left and the scale at the bottom right. mirrored swaps left and right, legendRight swaps the legend and the scale, and legendTop swaps the legend and the locator. "arrangement" also takes a corner for each ({"locator": "top-left", "northArrow": "top-right", "legend": "bottom-left", "scaleBar": "bottom-right"}). A surround over what the map draws in its corner (its layers and labels, not the basemap; more than 0.5 % of the surround) moves off the corner, to the freest place in the frame. It keeps 2 mm clear of the others, stays near its corner, and lines up with the frame's edges or centre or with another surround. The largest is placed first (the legend, as a rule), so it takes the freest place, and a smaller one whose corner it took moves out of its way. Your favourite arrangement, set with layout.arrangement {"favourite": …} and kept with the app's preferences, is the one tried first. Arrangements of your own are saved by name with layout.arrangement {"save": "Atlas", "arrangement": {…}} (up to 20, kept with the preferences too) and removed with {"remove": "Atlas"}; "arrangement": "Atlas" then takes one as it takes a built-in one, and a favourite can name one. "arrangement": "best", the default, lays out the favourite, every arrangement by name and every saved one, and keeps the one that moves the least (by area, so moving the north arrow costs less than moving the legend), then the one leaving the least over the data; the favourite wins a tie. "favourite" always takes the favourite. The answer names the arrangement taken ("arrangement") and the surrounds moved off their corners ("moved"). Where nowhere is freer, a surround stays. One warning names those left with more than a quarter of their area on the data while a corner of their size had more than a quarter less, to move by hand if that matters (data under every corner alike, such as a context layer filling the frame, isn't worth a warning); two that overlap (a frame too small) are said too. To see why a surround moved or stayed, "coverage": "grid.png" writes the grid the placement searched: what the map draws dark, the free cells white, the strips the graticule's labels keep tinted blue, and the surrounds placed over the map outlined in red. It shows the map's frame only: what sits outside it (a side column's locator and legend, as "style": "panel" places them, the titles and the footer) isn't on it. The top visible raster with a colour scale, not drawn by classes (Stretch, a tinted hillshade), gets a colorbar beneath the legend; RGB and untinted hillshade do not, so an RGB composite over a DEM leaves it to the DEM. A raster drawn by classes (Colormap, Unique Values, Classify) has its classes in the legend's rows instead, so a map whose only theme is a land cover gets a filled legend and no colorbar; a colour table over more values than a legend lists (over 256, or not whole) has neither, and a warning says so. A raster on a ramp on top that is still reading gets no colorbar either, rather than the one of a raster under it, and a warning says so too. The colorbar's title is the raster's name: the legend's box widens for it, up to its cap (45% of the frame's width, or the side column's), and a longer name wraps there, the box taller by its lines. "colorbar": "none" leaves it out, and the legend's box is sized for the legend alone: hiding the colorbar afterwards would leave its room empty in the box. The legend takes the fewest columns, up to three, that fit the largest rectangle the map leaves free; when none fits, a long one is laid in more when that shows more of it, or as much while covering at least a quarter less of what the map draws (the answer's legendColumns); in a side column it stays in one. Polygon layers with no fill and no labels, such as neighbouring borders, don't count as what the map draws here, unless nothing else is shown, nor do polygon layers in one colour with no labels that run on past the frame, such as neighbouring states, beside a layer drawn by classes or a raster. When no shape of the legend fits, the map is first moved towards an edge, by a quarter, a half, three quarters or all of the way the asked extent can go and stay in the frame, so the room on both its sides joins (the answer's mapShift, in mm right and down, and a warning). Of the moves that need the fewest legend columns, the one that leaves the extent's centre nearest the page's visual centre (in the middle across, 45% of the way down) is kept: a move away from that centre goes only as far as makes room, one towards it as far as keeps it nearest. Of the moves within 1 mm of the nearest, the one with the most room is kept. Where the projection stretches, as Web Mercator does away from the equator, the moved frame is drawn at its new centre's ground scale, and the extent has to stay whole at that scale. Only when no move makes room for it, a continuous map with a long legend is veiled: at the asked scale, at least 80% of the extent's part of the frame and 30% of the rest are drawn (not counting the layers just named), and the legend's box in one column, its colorbar included, is taller than a quarter of the frame; a sparser map stays unveiled. The veil covers what is outside the extent's bounding box in the map's projection, but for the strips under the graticule's labels (so an extent whose edges curve there, as in a conic or polar projection, keeps the box's corners outside it unveiled), and the surrounds group in its widest band (the answer's veiled, arrangement "veil" and moved, and a warning), at up to about half the scale on a sheet (each scale tried is rounded up to two significant figures) when the frame leaves too little of it, which the result says too. When neither makes room, the map stays centred and unveiled, and the legend is laid out as above. The veil and the surrounds are ordinary elements, to move or delete as any other; "veil": false leaves the map unveiled and at its scale. If the legend or colorbar cannot fit, the result warns about clipping or resizing. A streamed layer still loading gets a fixed-size legend to adjust by hand.
  • Context and north: the layers the placement takes as context around the data (polygons only outlined, and polygons in one colour that run on past the frame beside a layer drawn by classes or a raster) are left out of the legend too, which a warning names; "contextInLegend": true lists them. A name is left out only when every layer shown under it is context. When the extent spans 20° or more on its longer side (its width taken at its middle latitude), the layout has no north arrow: the graticule gives north there, and one arrow can't point north across the whole frame. A warning says so; "northArrow": "always" draws one, and "none" leaves it out at any span.
  • On the globe: with the map on the Globe projection and no extent, the sheet shows the globe whole. The globe nearly fills the frame, centred where the view looks, and the surrounds go in the room around it. It has no scale bar, since a globe's scale holds only at its centre. A sheet has no locator by default either, since it would only show the same globe again, smaller; "locator": "globe" or "inset" still adds one. Warnings say both. Near a pole the frame can't draw the globe that small at the view's latitude, so the globe is centred as near it as fits, and a warning says where. With an extent, a sheet on the globe is laid out as on a flat map. In any frame on the globe, the light space around the globe is room for the surrounds, as the blank page is.
  • Legend box: over the map, the legend's box is left clear where the map shows nothing under it but its light plain paper or water: no layer, label, context layer, basemap shape or graticule line, no web tiles, and no veil. Dark basemaps keep a white backing so the dark legend text stays readable. Over light water its text sits on the page as on open sea. The answer's legendBox says whether it has one; the clear box is still an element, to fill by hand, and "legendBox": "always" keeps it white. A legend in a side column or on a card keeps its box (legendBox is true).
  • Decimal comma: "decimal": "," prints the graticule, scale bar and colorbar numbers and {scale} with a decimal comma and points between thousands (0,05, 1:6.300.000). layout.page={"decimal": ","} sets it on any layout; "." restores decimal points. This changes generated numbers, not numbers typed into text.
  • Texts: the title, subtitle, footer lines and authorship are measured with the layout's font (the title and headings bold, as drawn) and wrapped to their box, so none runs past it, and each box grows to hold them. {projection}, {datum} and {grid} are filled from the map when the layout is made. {date} and the other dynamic text tokens stay live. The reference defaults to the projection, datum and graticule, and the authorship to "Made with RustGIS · {date}" ("author": "" leaves it out). headings renames the two columns.
  • The locator (at the frame's top left unless the data is there): "locator": "globe" (a sheet's default) adds a separate globe map with simplified copies of the map's visible polygon layers held in the project. Streamed layers, rasters, points, lines and labels are left out. What simplifying changes or leaves alone there (a vertex past a pole taken to the pole, a polygon left as it was for a missing coordinate, or too intricate to simplify whole) is said in the layout's warnings; one kept as it was because its ring would collapse is not. The globe is whole in its circle: near a pole, where it can't be drawn that small at the area's latitude, it is centred as near the area as fits, the area still outlined on it, and the warnings say so. A locator too small for the whole globe anywhere (a figure's, under about 20 mm), or one that would have to move so far that the area went behind the globe's rim, stays centred on the area, its circle clipping the globe, which the warnings also say. It uses the map's basemap, not its web tiles, in a round frame, the globe nearly filling it and the area outlined in red; "inset" shows the surrounding region (about four times the area across) on the map itself, outlined the same way; "none" (a figure's default) leaves it out. The legend stops short of it; a frame with no room for a legend beside it leaves the legend out, with a warning. Undo takes the globe's map away with the layout; a globe map no layout shows any more (its layout deleted) is replaced by the next sheet's rather than left beside it, which a warning says.
  • The style ("style", on any sheet, portrait or landscape): "classic" (the default) sets the title over the map and the footer's boxes under it; "banner" puts the title and subtitle in a band of the accent colour across the top, the title in white; "panel" sets the map at the left and a pale panel at the right with the title, subtitle, locator and legend, and the sources and reference at its foot, the note across the bottom; "editorial" sets the title large in serif and a column at the right under a rule, with a heading (the third of headings), the note, the locator and the legend; "modular" sets cards with rounded corners on a light page: the map in one, the locator and the legend in one each beside it, the sources and reference in tinted ones under them; "poster" colours the whole page, sets the title and subtitle in light text across the top, the map in a light card and the footer in darker blocks with amber headings. "accent" (#RRGGBB) colours the band, the panel's tint, the headings and rules, and a poster's page (a deep brown, green or blue by default). A figure leaves the style to its caption. In the menu, each sheet opens a list of the six styles, each saying what it looks like, in its default accent ("accent" in the command picks another).
  • Every element is an ordinary one, to move or restyle afterwards. The command returns element IDs for layout.update, and warnings. Running it again with another profile makes another layout of the same map.
layout.publication={"profile": "a4-portrait", "extent": [-46.8, -11.6, -38.5, -2.2],
  "title": "PIAUÍ", "subtitle": "Greening and browning of vegetation: NDVI trend, 2001–2025",
  "sources": ["NDVI: MOD13Q1 v6.1 (NASA LP DAAC), via the Brazil Data Cube (INPE)", "Boundaries: IBGE 2022"],
  "note": "Only cells with a significant trend (p < 0.05) are coloured."}
  • Map frames: rectangle, circle and ellipse frames, or polygon and lasso frames drawn on the page. Each frame shows a map of the project at its own centre and scale. Turn on Pan/Zoom Map (Map Frames group, or a frame's properties) to drag inside frames to pan their maps and turn the wheel to zoom them about the pointer; Esc turns it off. Without it, Shift-drag a frame to pan its map. Double-click a frame to match the map view, or set the scale in its properties: from the whole world down to site plans at 1:20, finds at 1:1 and 2:1 (a scale of 1:0.5). A frame can be turned and tilted like the map view: a new frame, or one matched to the view, takes the view's bearing and pitch, and Bearing and Pitch in its properties (or layout.update with bearing and pitch) set them. Its north arrows turn with it. Grid draws a labelled graticule over a frame; Extent Indicator outlines another frame's extent (an overview map).
  • Frame borders: the Border section of a frame's properties sets the line drawn round it, along its shape for a round or polygon frame: its Color and Line width (dark grey and 0.8 pt to begin with, up to 20 pt). The line is centred on the frame's edge, as a rectangle's outline is, so a wide border reaches half its width past the frame. A width of 0 draws no border, so a frame on a coloured page (a blank basemap with a paper colour) blends into it; Default Border brings the dark grey line back. Scripts set the same with layout.update and "border": {"color": "#C8B89A", "width": 0} (the keys not given keep theirs; null gives the default back).
  • Extent indicators: the Extent Indicator section of an overview frame's properties sets the outline's Color and Line width (red and 2 pt to begin with), so it can sit quietly on a muted page, and Remove Extent Indicator takes it away. Scripts set the same with color and width on layout.addElement (kind extentIndicator), or layout.update with "extentIndicator": {"color": "#555555", "width": 1} (false removes it). The outline breaks where the other frame's edge leaves the Earth (off a globe's disc, or past the edge of its projection's world) and runs up to it there, and where the overview's own projection can't show the place. An outline across the overview map's edge (on a world projection, the meridian opposite its central one) is cut there, each piece on its side of the map, and so is a frame on the globe around a pole outlined on Web Mercator, at ±180°.
  • Grids: the Grid section of a frame's properties turns the grid on and sets its Interval in degrees (Auto picks one from the frame's extent), its Style (full lines across the frame, ticks along its neatline only, or both, with the Tick length), its colour and Line width, and its labels: decimal degrees (112.5°W, as many decimals as the interval needs) or degrees, minutes and seconds (112°30′W), with a hemisphere letter or a sign (-112.5°), on any of the four edges (longitudes along the top and bottom, latitudes along the left and right; the bottom and left to start with), the side labels horizontal or running along the edge, and their Label size ("labelSize", 4 to 100 points, 8 by default; their room from the frame's edge and a measured grid's MGRS square names follow in proportion, labels that would then touch, or that reach further in than the frame is across, are left out, and null gives the default back). From a command: layout.addElement={"kind":"grid","step":0.5,"style":"ticks","format":"dms","edges":["top","bottom","left","right"],"labelOrientation":"vertical"} (given again, it changes only the options it names), or layout.update with "grid": {…} (false removes the grid). On a map on the globe the lines are drawn on the globe, and stop at its horizon. A frame that shows the whole globe has nothing beyond its rim, and so no labels along its edges.
  • Measured grids: a grid's Type is a Graticule (latitudes and longitudes, as above) or a Measured grid, as ArcGIS Pro has them: eastings and northings in a projected coordinate system, for maps read with UTM or national grid coordinates (a hiking map at 1:25,000 with a line every kilometre). Its Coordinate system is the one typed (EPSG:25832, WKT or a PROJ string); left empty, the map's own when the map is in a projected system, else the UTM zone (WGS 84) at the frame's centre. Its Interval is in that system's units, metres for UTM (Auto picks 1, 2 or 5 times a power of ten that suits the frame; one too fine for the frame is widened to about 50 lines). Its lines are drawn through the coordinate system onto the map, so on a map in another projection they lean or bend as they do there; eastings are labelled along the top and bottom and northings along the sides, whole (543000) or, with Short labels, as topographic maps print them, the kilometres' last two digits (43). Style, ticks, colour, width, edges and side label orientation are as for a graticule. A system that isn't projected (a latitude/longitude one) is refused: that is a graticule's job. From a command: layout.update={"element":1,"grid":{"type":"measured","crs":"EPSG:25832","step":1000,"short":true}}; a change of type without a step picks one, as degrees and metres don't carry over.
  • MGRS labels: a measured grid's MGRS labels name each 100 km square that shows with its grid zone designation and square letters (32T MT), in the middle of what shows of it, and label the lines by their kilometres' last two digits (05; whole, at an interval of 100 km or more), as military and rescue maps print them; Auto picks an interval of at most 100 km, so the squares' edges are drawn (on a frame over 10,000 km across, where a 100 km grid would be a blur, it is widened as for any measured grid). The grid is then in a WGS 84 UTM zone: the one typed as its EPSG code (EPSG:32632, or 32601–32660 north and 32701–32760 south), else the map's own when the map is in one, else MGRS's zone at the frame's centre (southern Norway's 32V and Svalbard's wider zones included). Another system is refused while MGRS labels are on, and a UTM zone typed as WKT or a PROJ string isn't read as one; a switch to a graticule turns them off. A square is named where all of its label fits in it, clear of the edge labels: a frame too small for one names none, and nor does a frame north of 84° N or south of 80° S, where MGRS leaves UTM for the polar grids (UPS), though its lines are drawn. Across the Equator, the squares on the other side are named as MGRS names them there. On a frame across a zone's edge, only the zone the grid is in has its squares named, as the lines past its edge are that zone's eastings and northings carried on, not the next zone's. From a command: layout.update={"element":1,"grid":{"type":"measured","mgrs":true}}. A project with MGRS labels on a graticule or naming another system (a hand-edited file) opens with them off, and says so.
  • Map surrounds: north arrow, scale bar, legend, colorbar, table frame (a layer's attribute table) and chart frame (a bar chart of a numeric field), plus a neatline or backdrop. A north arrow's properties pick its Style (classic arrow, compass rose, triangle or star) and its Fill and Outline colours ("style":"compass","fill":"#B91C1C"). A scale bar's pick its Style (alternating bar, stepped line or single line), its Units (metres or kilometres to suit the scale, or kilometres, metres, miles or feet), its Divisions (1 to 10), each labelled where there is room, its Color (the lines, the dark divisions and the labels), its Fill (the alternating bar's light divisions) and its Text size ("textSize", 4 to 100 points, 8 by default; the room over the bar and between the labels follows, the bar keeps its length and height and moves down, and right, in its box to make room for larger labels, which reach no further past its top and left than the default size's, and null gives the default back) ("style":"line","units":"miles","divisions":2,"color":"#1F4E79","textSize":12). A box too small for its labels and bar (too short, which draws the bar below it, too narrow for its last label, or so narrow that a larger last label runs into the first), or a label that runs off the page, is said when its place, size, text size, units or divisions change or its frame is drawn at another scale, and an export reports it as cut off, as it does a text. A locked scale bar refuses a new text size, null included. A legend's parts:
  • What a legend lists: what its frame draws: only layers with a feature at the frame's scale (through their definition query, display filters and scale range) and, for Unique Values, only the classes those features have, with <all other values> only when some feature falls in it and its symbol is not fully transparent. Graduated Colors and Proportional Symbols list their classes as they are (they are taken from the kept features when the symbology is applied). A layer that draws nothing there, such as an empty one, is left out, in layouts saved before this too. Tick All classes in its properties ("allClasses": true) to list every class of every visible layer instead, and Hide other values ("hideOther": true) to leave <all other values> out of the legend in either mode (the features it stands for are still drawn).
  • Show no data ("showNoData": true on layout.addElement or layout.update) adds a No data row for a Graduated Colors layer's missing numeric values, using the same symbol as the map. It follows the frame's definition query and display filters; with All classes, it lists the symbol even when unused. It is off by default, including in older saved layouts. It changes only the legend; other renderer kinds keep their entries.
  • Rasters drawn by classes (Colormap, Unique Values, Classify) are listed under their name with the rows the Layers pane shows for them: a swatch of each class's colour and its label, the class name in the file when it has one, else the value or the range. The legend lists the classes the raster holds (the values in its statistics, without those its mask hides). On a raster with too many values to list (over 256, or not whole) it lists every Unique Values class, and for Classify the classes from its lowest value to its highest. With All classes it lists every class of a Unique Values or Classify renderer. A colour table lists the values the raster holds either way: its 256 entries have no meaningful "all". The rows are the Layers pane's, and the legend also lists them under a mask where the pane doesn't yet: a colour table over too many values lists those its mask leaves when they are few enough. Rasters on a ramp (Stretch, a tinted hillshade) and RGB composites are left out: a colorbar keys the ramp. Exclude leaves a classified raster out by its name. A cloud-optimised GeoTIFF read in place keeps an overview, and its classes are those of the overview, which can miss a rare one the map draws at full resolution; so can the classes of a raster under a mask with more than about four million cells, whose masked statistics read a sample of them: adding the legend, and layout.publication, say so.
  • Columns ("columns", 1 to 3): its rows side by side under its title, as even as they go, each column as wide as its widest row, with its own column of symbols, and a layer's name staying with the row under it. The box keeps its size, so widen it for the columns: in a box too narrow they close up and long labels run into the next or past the box, and a box too short fills each column in turn and leaves out the last rows. A legend says when its box loses rows or closes its columns up when it is given "columns", "textSize" (null included), "showNoData" or "labels", and, in columns or at a text size of its own, when its box or what it lists changes. A legend in one column at the default size is told so only when given one of those.
  • Text size ("textSize", 4 to 100 points, 9 by default; a size outside that range is refused): the size of its layers' names and classes' labels. Its title, rows, indents and the gap between columns follow in proportion, and so do a web layer's legend pictures and the length and height of line and polygon swatches, whose lines keep the width the map draws them at (a swatch's line or outline that reaches past its row is cut at the row's top and bottom, so it doesn't cover the rows beside it, and at its sides where its text starts, the column before it or the legend's edge begins, so it doesn't cover its label, another column or what lies past the legend). A point symbol keeps the size the map draws it at, its row growing only when the symbol needs it, so a legend of proportional symbols still matches the map. "textSize": null gives the default back. A size its box no longer holds says what the legend loses, and so does a box made too small for a legend with a size of its own (see Columns for when a legend says so).
  • Point symbols: a point layer's symbol is drawn as the map draws it: its marker or the stacked marks of its class or layer, its size and outline, the transparency of the layer and its groups, and its reference scale at the frame's scale, unturned. A row has one symbol, so it leaves out what changes from feature to feature (a symbol by expression, a marker's rotation by a field), and it doesn't take the layer's blend mode. The row and the column of symbols grow to hold a large one, up to 2048 points, past which the symbol is cut. A legend already on the page keeps its size, so with a larger symbol its last rows may be cut and its labels run past its right edge: make it larger to fit.
  • Line and polygon symbols: a line's swatch is drawn with the stacked strokes of its class or layer (casings, offsets, dashes, markers and images along it, by symbol level when the layer draws by levels), and a polygon's with its stacked fills (hatches, dots, gradients, shapeburst), as the map draws them, with the transparency of the layer and its groups and its reference scale at the frame's scale. A stroke wider than the row, or shifted off it, is cut at the row; the row keeps its height. A diagram's categories keep their own colour. A layer with no stacked strokes or fills keeps its plain swatch, see-through with the layer.
  • Web layers: a visible WMS or WMTS layer with a legend is listed by its name over its service's legend picture, the one for the frame's scale (as the Layers pane shows it), a pixel a point and narrowed to fit the legend's width, in the order the map draws its layers (as the Layers pane lists them); one whose picture can't be loaded (or is wider or taller than 2048 pixels) is left out. The Layout view does not wait for pictures on either desktop or browser: it draws what has arrived, then redraws once the pending legend pictures finish, not on every unrelated download. Desktop exports wait up to half a minute in all for each page's legend pictures (each page of a map series has its own wait), as they wait for the map's tiles. A page drawn while a legend picture is still on its way stops the export, even if the picture lands afterwards, rather than leave the file silently incomplete. The browser cannot wait: export again once the pictures have arrived.
  • Its Layers section: a checkbox for each layer of the frame's map (and each WMS or WMTS layer with a legend): untick one to leave it out of the legend while the map still draws it (a mask, a highlight, a basemap-like layer). Scripts give the same with "exclude": ["Graticule"] on layout.addElement or layout.update ([] lists every layer again); a renamed layer stays left out.
  • Labels: a legend can show a layer under a label instead of its name, so a layer named for a plugin or a script (BrasilData: Setores censitários 2022 (MG), setores_mg_2022_clip) reads well on the page while it keeps its name everywhere else. Scripts give "labels": {"BrasilData: Setores censitários 2022 (MG)": "Setores censitários"} on layout.addElement or layout.update: the layer by its name (any case), as exclude names it, and a layer the map hasn't is refused. Each entry replaces that layer's label and leaves the others; null or a blank label shows the layer's name again, and {} every layer's. The label goes in the layer's row, or over its classes; a WMS or WMTS layer's goes over its picture. It is up to 200 characters (a longer one is refused), and the legend measures it, not the name, so sizing a legend to its content and the warnings on its fit follow the label. Two legends can label a layer differently (in two languages, say). A renamed layer keeps its label (not when another layer has its new name, which is said); a removed one's is dropped, so a later layer of that name isn't labelled unasked. Labels are saved with the layout, in the project and in a .rgislayout file.
  • Colorbar: the colour scale of a raster layer in the frame's map, as the map draws it (its stretch, colour ramp and enhancement): a gradient bar for a stretch or a tinted hillshade, steps labelled at their breaks for Classify, and a block per value for Unique Values and colour tables. Its properties pick the Layer (unless one is picked, the top visible raster with a colour scale, not drawn by classes: a legend lists a land cover's classes, and a colorbar added over a map with only such rasters draws an empty placeholder and says so), the Title (the units, such as Elevation (m); the layer's name to start with; a title wider than the colorbar wraps at spaces onto more lines, with the bar under them), the Orientation, which side the Labels go on, how many Ticks a gradient labels (2 to 21, the ends included) and Reverse (high values first). An RGB raster or a hillshade without a tint has no colour scale and shows a placeholder, as does a picked layer that was renamed or removed (it is kept by name, like a table or chart frame's layer) until another is picked. From a command: layout.addElement={"kind":"colorbar","layer":"DEM","title":"Elevation (m)","orientation":"vertical","ticks":3}.
  • Shapes: Rectangle, Circle, Ellipse and Line add a shape in the middle of the page, to frame, highlight or divide parts of it. The Line menu starts a horizontal, vertical or diagonal line; turn it to any angle with its rotation handle or properties. A circle is an ellipse in a square box. The properties set a shape's fill, outline and outline width (0 for none; a fill with no opacity leaves only the outline), a rectangle's rounded corners ("radius", in mm), and a line's colour, width, arrowheads and angle. From a command: layout.addElement={"kind":"line","dir":"falling","color":"#C0392B","width":2}. A colour goes only to the elements that draw it, and is refused for the others. color is a text's, a line's, an arrow's or a scale bar's, and sets a shape's outline unless outline is also given; when adding, it is also an extent indicator's or a grid's. fill is a north arrow's, a shape's or a scale bar's, and outline a north arrow's or a shape's. A legend, picture, table and the like take none. A line and the outline of a rectangle, circle or ellipse can be dashed: Dash in their properties offers the Symbology pane's patterns (Solid, Dashed, Dotted, Dash-dot, Long dash), scaled by the width as there, for a dashed leader, a box marking a study area or an inset's extent, or a dotted neatline. On an arrow only the shaft is dashed; its heads stay solid. A command gives the pattern as a line layer's stroke takes it, lengths in points, dash and gap in turn (at most 8, each up to 500): layout.update={"element":7,"dash":[4,2]}, with [] or null for solid again. Every export draws it, SVG as a stroke-dasharray.
  • Arrows: Arrow adds an arrow pointing right. Drag the round handle above a selected arrow or line to turn it about its centre: it snaps to level, upright and diagonal within 3°, and Shift turns it in 15° steps. Angle in its properties sets it exactly (0° points right, 90° up), and Arrowheads puts heads at its end, its start, both or neither, so any line can be an arrow. From a command: layout.addElement={"kind":"arrow","angle":30,"length":60}, or layout.update={"element":7,"angle":-90} to turn one.
  • Text clipping: the layout view and PNG, PDF and SVG exports warn when a text element or legend title runs off the page, or a text element has more lines than its height allows. The notice counts and names the affected elements with their IDs. A line may extend beyond its element's width while remaining on the page; that alone is not clipped. Reduce the size, add line breaks, turn on Wrap to the box, or resize or move the element to keep all its text; when every text it names is already wrapped, the notice suggests only the size and the box, and a legend's title, which has no wrap, is told to add line breaks. Lines left out below a text's box are said as soon as they are: layout.addElement and layout.update warn how many lines don't fit when they set a text, its size, font or fallback font, bold, italic, alignment, spacing, wrap or box, or show it, and the Format pane shows the count under Wrap to the box (a text wrapped past the 200 lines a text draws counts them as "or more"). A text draws 200 lines at most, whatever its box: lines past them are said apart, with the advice to split the text into more than one, since a taller box doesn't draw them.
  • Text and Dynamic Text: {date}, {time}, {scale}, {map}, {crs}, {page}, {pages}, {pageName} and {layout} fill in when the page is drawn; the Format pane's Insert Dynamic Text menu, under the text, lists each with what it shows. The text, its size (2 to 400 points, as text graphics on the map), colour, bold, Italic (slanted, as water and ocean names are; the built-in fonts have no italic face, so the letters are slanted 12°), Alignment (left, center or right within the element) and Letter spacing in points (spaced-out names such as O C E A N O, or a negative value to tighten) are the same form a text on the map has in the pane; Enter ends the text and Shift+Enter starts a new line. Scripts set them with italic, align and letterSpacing on layout.addElement and layout.update. A layout's text also takes a Font: Sans-serif (the default) or Serif, for a title or a classic atlas look. The serif is one installed on the computer (Georgia or Times New Roman on Windows and macOS, Noto, DejaVu or Liberation Serif on Linux), with its own bold and italic faces, so italic there is a true italic rather than slanted letters (a family with no bold or italic file draws that style from its regular face: italic slanted, bold as the regular); RustGIS bundles no font. The project saves only the family, so it opens anywhere. Where no serif is installed (the web app, a minimal Linux), the text is drawn in the sans-serif and the pane says so, as layout.addElement and layout.update do in a warning. Scripts set it with "font": "serif" or "font": "sans".

The Font list also has every font family installed on the computer, after Sans-serif and Serif, so a figure can match the typeface a journal or a series names (Times New Roman, Arial, Garamond, a condensed face for long names). Type part of a name to find one. Its bold and italic come from the family's own faces, as for the serif, and characters it lacks from the sans. A named family keeps a Fallback, Sans-serif or Serif, for a computer that doesn't have it (a project opened elsewhere, the web app): there the text is drawn in the fallback, and the pane and the commands say so. A family that is installed but whose file can't be read (too large, damaged) is drawn in the fallback too, and the command that names it says so. The fallback starts as the family's own class (a serif's is Serif). A text turns with Rotation, in degrees clockwise about the centre of its box, as a map frame's bearing turns: 90 reads top to bottom and -90 bottom to top, for a source line up the margin, a vertical coordinate or a name along a river. The 0°, 90° and -90° buttons beside it set those. The element keeps its unturned box (X, Y, Width, Height), so moving and aligning work as before; its selection and handles follow the turned box. Every export draws it turned (PNG, PDF, GeoPDF, SVG). Scripts set "rotation": 90 on layout.addElement and layout.update; an angle outside -180 to 180 is kept as the same turn within it (450 is 90). Wrap to the box breaks a text into lines at spaces to fit the width of its box, for a note, a source line or a caption: the lines follow the box when it is resized and the text when it is edited or its size changes, so there are no line breaks to put in by hand. It is off by default, so a text breaks lines only where it has a line break, as before. Dynamic text wraps once filled in, the text's own line breaks are kept, a word wider than the box (a long address) is broken across lines, a no-break space keeps its words on one line, and a turned text wraps to the width of its unturned box. The layout view and every export (PNG, PDF, SVG) break the same lines. Spaces running together in a wrapped text are drawn as one, and no ordinary space is kept at a line's start or end. Lines that don't fit the box's height are left out, and the commands, the Format pane and the export notice above say so. Scripts set "wrap": true on layout.addElement and layout.update. A project with a wrapped text needs this release or a later one to open. Scripts give the family's name: "font": "Garamond", with "fallback": "serif" if wanted; labels.fonts lists the installed families. The project keeps the name, never a file. A PDF sets its text in the letters' own outlines, so it shows the family wherever it is opened, and the text can still be searched and copied; no font file goes into it. Picture places a PNG or JPEG. - Map Series: one page per feature of an index layer; see Map series for setup, page names, scales and a locator. - Click an element to select it, drag to move, drag its handles to resize, and edit it in the Format pane, which opens as a tab beside the other right-hand panes. Closing the pane keeps the element selected; selecting an element, or clicking the selected one, opens it again. The wheel zooms the page, and each layout keeps its own zoom and pan while the project is open. Delete (or Backspace, the delete key on a Mac) deletes the selected element, and Ctrl+D (⌘D on a Mac) duplicates it: the copy is placed 5 mm down and to the right, on top, and selected. The arrow keys nudge it 1 mm, or 10 mm with Shift, and keep it moving while held; a run of nudges is one step to undo. - Arrange: elements later in the layout are drawn over earlier ones, and a click picks the one on top. Under Duplicate and Delete, Bring to Front and Send to Back put the selected element over or under every other, and Bring Forward and Send Backward move it one place, to put a legend over a map frame, say. A locked element keeps its place. From a command: layout.arrange={"element":4,"to":"front"} (front, forward, backward or back). - Locked, at the top of the Format pane, keeps a finished element from being changed by mistake: it can still be selected (a lock marks its corner, and it has no handles), but not moved, resized, deleted or edited, and a locked map frame's map isn't panned or zoomed. A map series still turns its pages. Duplicate makes an unlocked copy, and a map frame isn't deleted while one of its surrounds is locked. Scripts lock and unlock with layout.update {"locked": true} or {"locked": false}. - Background: every element but a map frame can have a box drawn behind it, which moves, resizes, hides and turns with it, in the app and in PDF, PNG and SVG exports: a light box under a legend or a scale bar over a busy map, or under a text on a dark page. Turn on Background in the Format pane, then pick its Fill and Outline colours, the Outline width (0 for none), the Corner radius and the Padding, how far the box reaches past the element on every side; the element itself is drawn as before. It starts white with no outline, and a duplicate keeps it. From a command: layout.update={"element":4,"background":{"fill":"#FFFFFFCC","radius":1,"padding":1.5}}; the keys not given keep theirs, and "background":null removes it. layout.addElement takes it too. A map frame draws its own map and paper, so a background on one is refused. - Ink: a legend, a colorbar, a table and a chart draw their text and lines in dark grey; turn on Ink in the Format pane and pick its colour to read them on a dark page or over imagery. It starts white over a dark background and dark grey otherwise, and turned off it is the dark grey again. A legend's title, layer names and class labels, a colorbar's title, outline, ticks and labels, a table's text and a chart's title and labels take it; a chart's bars and a colorbar's colours keep theirs. A table with an ink draws no white cells of its own, only its header and every other row in a light tint of the ink, so it reads on whatever is behind it: give it a Background for a box. From a command: layout.update={"element":5,"color":"#FFFFFF"}, and "color":null for the default; layout.addElement takes it too. - Chart frame: a bar chart of a layer of the frame's map. Its properties pick the Layer and the numeric Field, and by default draw a bar per feature, the largest first, each labelled by the Label field. Group by a field draws a bar per value of that field instead, its Statistic of the field over that value's features: sum, count (features, numbers or not, so a count can chart a text field), mean, minimum or maximum. Values are grouped as they are stored, capitals and spaces included: "North" and "north " are two bars. Features with no value, or one that is only spaces, are one "(blank)" bar. A text field charted that way gives way to the layer's first numeric field when the chart stops grouping or counting, and the chart says so. Order puts the largest first, the smallest first, the labels A to Z or the bars in the layer's order. Bars sets how many are drawn (1 to 100, 20 by default); Past the limit leaves the rest out, which the chart says, or gathers them in a last Other bar, its statistic over their features (their sum when not grouped), last whatever the order. A negative value is drawn the other way from a line at zero. Features with no number in the field are left out, and the chart says how many; a count counts them, since it reads no number. From a command: layout.update={"element":6,"group":"region","statistic":"mean","order":"category","bars":8,"other":true}, and "group":"" for a bar per feature again; layout.addElement takes them too. - Chart look: Orientation draws bars running across (the default) or Vertical columns standing on the bottom, each label centred under its column and cut to its width. Title is the one the chart is given (its field, or the statistic of its field by the group), None, which leaves its room to the bars, or Own, typed in Title text. Values writes each bar's value past its end, its thousands apart with commas (points with a layout's decimal comma), with up to two Decimals by default, the zeros they end in dropped, or a fixed 0 to 6; on narrow columns, or beside bars they would leave less than half the chart, they are written smaller to fit. Bar color sets the bars' colour. Color by class draws each bar in the colour its features have on the map, by the layer's symbology: a bar per feature in its feature's, a grouped bar in the colour its features share, so grouping by the field the layer's Unique Values class by gives each bar its class's colour. The colour is a symbol's fill (an outline's where the fill is clear, a line's stroke), by each feature's own geometry, as the chart's map frame draws it (at its scale, and on its series page), not its patterns or stacked strokes, nor the layer's transparency: the bars are drawn whole. Features of a hidden class, or points drawn as an image, show none and leave the bar to the others. A bar whose features have several colours or show none, and an Other bar, keep the bar colour, and the chart says how many bars do, and why, when it is added or changed. The bars follow the symbology when it changes; those counts are not said again then. The bars take the layer's colours whether the frame draws the layer or not: a layer turned off, or outside its visible scale range, still colours them. Labels puts the labels of bars running across at the left of their column, or By the bars, against them. Keep a title out of the chart to style it as you like with a text element. From a command: layout.update={"element":6,"orientation":"vertical","values":true,"decimals":0,"title":"","barColor":"#C2185B","labelAlign":"right"}, "title":null for the title it is given again and "barColor":null for the default blue; "classes":true colours the bars by class; layout.addElement takes them too. - Stacked bars: a chart that groups and sums or counts can Stack by another field. Each bar is then split by that field's values, a stretch per value, and a key under the title names them: the population of each continent split by income group, for example. The stretches add up to the bar, so the row is shown only for a sum or a count. The key lists the values the bars drawn show, by their total over them, the largest first (A to Z when the bars go by label); past 8 values, it keeps the 7 with the largest totals and gathers the rest in a grey Other, and the chart says how many it gathers. The stretches take the colours diagrams take, or, with Color by class, the colour each value's features share on the map, so stacking by the field the layer's Unique Values class by gives each stretch its class's colour; a value whose features have several colours, or show none, keeps its palette colour, and the chart says how many do. Stretches below zero run the other way from the zero line, and Values writes the bar's total. An Other bar is stacked too, from the bars it gathers. A box too short to leave the bars room under the title and key shows the chart's placeholder, and the chart says so when it is added, resized, retitled or turned (not when the layer's data changes): make it taller. A stack field deleted from the layer leaves the bars whole, and the chart's next change lets the stack go, saying so. Turning grouping off, taking a mean, minimum or maximum, or moving the chart to a layer without the field lets the stack go, and the chart says so. From a command: layout.update={"element":6,"group":"continent","stack":"income_grp"}, and "stack":"" for whole bars again; layout.addElement takes it too. - Rulers and guides: rulers start on along the top and left and show millimetres from the page's corner. Drag out of a ruler to add a guide, drag a guide to move it, and drag it back onto a ruler to remove it. Guides are saved with the layout but never printed or exported. Clear Guides removes them all, and Rulers in the Page group hides the rulers. - Snap starts on: a dragged or resized element snaps to the guides, the page's edges and centre, and the other elements' edges and centres, and the line it snapped to is highlighted. Hold Alt to place an element freely, or turn Snap off in the Page group. - Export writes PDF (every map series page by default, up to 500), or PNG or SVG of the current page. On desktop, Cancel also stops an export while it waits for legend pictures or map tiles; a cancelled export does not replace the destination file. A PDF keeps the page as vectors: text, shapes, surrounds and map frames (their features, labels and grids, turned with the frame) stay sharp at any zoom or print size, and linear and radial gradient fills are PDF gradients. What is pixels by nature is a picture in it at the export's dpi, keeping its layer's opacity and blend mode: rasters, web tile layers, pictures, legend images from map services, a masked layer, and a frame that is tilted, on the globe or with view effects. Hatch and dot fills, shapeburst fills, and sweeping, repeating or reflected gradients are pictures cut to their shape. The dpi also sets how fine the vectors' detail is kept, as a PNG at that resolution would show it. A frame or page whose vectors would pass 256 MB (64 MB in the browser) is drawn as a picture instead, and the export says which. Its text is set in the fonts it is drawn in, each embedded as a subset of its glyphs, so a vector editor that reads embedded fonts (VectorCraft, where it was checked) opens it as text that can be edited; it can be searched and copied in any PDF reader. A face is set as its letters' outlines instead, and the export names it with why, when its licence forbids embedding or subsetting or allows embedding bitmaps only; when it is drawn at another weight or width than its variable font file's default; when its file doesn't hold it as drawn; when its file can't be subset (a damaged file, or a CFF2 variable font); when its subset would pass 8 MB; or when the document's embedded faces would pass 32 MB. A label's halo is a copy of its text under it, filled and stroked as wide as the halo, so it is text too (marked as decoration, an artifact, for readers that leave those out of what they copy). "pdf": {"text": "outlines"} on layout.export sets all the text, halos too, as outlines, as a print shop asking for outlined type wants. Its page has layers (PDF optional content groups), which a vector editor that reads them opens as layers (VectorCraft, where it was checked), and PDF readers list in their layers panel: one for each element, named after it, and inside a map frame's, one for each map layer and one for each layer's labels (Labels: <layer>), and one for the frame's grid. A map series' pages share them. "pdf": {"layers": false} on layout.export leaves them out. The map view's PDF (Publish › Print Map) has its map layers and labels as layers too. A PDF is also a GeoPDF: each map frame is georeferenced (an ISO 32000 geospatial viewport, the form GDAL, QGIS, Acrobat and Avenza read), with its map's coordinate system as WKT and its EPSG code when it has one, so a reader shows coordinates and measures in it, and GDAL or QGIS open the page as a georeferenced raster. It carries coordinates only: no layers and no feature attributes. A tilted frame, one on the globe, and one with a corner off the Earth aren't georeferenced, and the export says which (in a map series, on which pages when not on all: each page moves the frame). Where a frame is drawn over another, only the one on top carries coordinates there (none when it isn't georeferenced), so a reader doesn't show the other's through it. A viewport is a rectangle, so a shaped frame (circle, ellipse, polygon) drawn over another is left out, and the frame under it has no coordinates in its box. A frame in a projection with no WKT form is georeferenced by its corners in longitude/latitude when that puts the places on a 9 × 9 grid over it within 0.5 mm of where they are drawn (measured there, not proven between them), and left out otherwise. Georeference PDF in the Publish tab's Export group ("georeference": false for layout.export) leaves the coordinates out. In SVG each element is a group (an Inkscape layer) named after it: text stays text and rectangles, ellipses and lines stay shapes, and map frames and the other surrounds are kept as vectors as in a PDF (their labels as the letters' shapes, each run labelled with its text), so the page can be finished in a vector editor. What a PDF keeps as pictures is a picture in the SVG too, at the export's dpi, and a frame whose vectors would pass the budget is drawn as a picture, with a notice. A picture marker's image is written once and placed with <use> wherever drawn, so a vector editor shows each place as a clone of it. A text element uses the viewer's font (a named family where the viewer has it, else its serif or sans-serif), so its alignment and line widths can differ from PNG/PDF and can change when edited. A picture standing for a whole element is clipped to its box; layout.export accepts dpi and "allPages": false for just the current PDF page. Print opens the PDF in the system viewer on desktop; Save Layout File and Import Layout move layouts between projects (.rgislayout). - Capture To Clipboard copies an image of the current page at 192 dpi, with any text still being typed. A page that would pass 16 million pixels, or 12,000 pixels on a side, is copied at a lower dpi, and the notice says so; a page that would need less than 36 dpi is too large to copy, so export it instead.

Map series¶

Select the main map frame, then Publish › Map Frames › Map Series and choose its index layer (tracts, for example). The series follows the layer's feature order. Previous Page and Next Page step through it; Refresh refits the current page after changes to the index layer.

Use layout.series to set a page-name field and margin; for example, on the active layout:

layout.series={"layer":"Tracts","nameField":"GEOID","margin":0.1}

Without frame (the frame's element id), the command uses the layout's first map frame, so name the frame when the series is on another one. Running layout.series again starts the series over at its first page. nameField supplies {pageName} in dynamic text; if omitted, it uses the index layer's label field, or the row number (starting at 1) when that field is unavailable or its value is null, empty or whitespace-only. Add a text element such as Tract {pageName} · {page} of {pages}. In commands, layout.seriesPage={"page":0} is the first page; the displayed {page} starts at 1.

The frame fits each feature's extent anew, so its scale varies by page. margin is a fraction of the feature's longitude/latitude extent added on each side: 0.1 (the default) adds 10% of its width on the left and right, and 10% of its height above and below, with at least 0.01° on each side. Bind a scale bar to the series frame; {scale} text uses the layout's first map frame, so keep the series on that frame when using it. A feature without geometry or with an empty extent leaves the frame at the preceding view.

For a locator, add a second map frame and pan/zoom it to the whole study area. Select that locator frame, then Publish › Map Frames › Extent Indicator and choose the main frame. The locator outlines the main frame's changing extent as the series turns its pages.

Export Layout writes all pages to PDF by default, but only the first 500 when there are more, with a warning. Split longer books' index data into layers of at most 500 features. layout.export with "allPages":false writes only the current PDF page; PNG and SVG always write the current page.

Mark the page's own feature¶

Set Symbols by Expression on the index layer. Use $id = @page_feature_id to give the current feature a heavier outline and draw the rest in gray. For an index layer called Tracts, the command is:

layer.symbolBy={"layer":"Tracts","strokeWidth":"if($id = @page_feature_id, 3, 0.6)","stroke":"if($id = @page_feature_id, '#174a7e', '#9ca3af')","fill":"if($id = @page_feature_id, '#8bbce8', '#e5e7eb')"}

Use a style rather than a definition query: a definition query such as $id = @page_feature_id leaves the layer empty in the map view, where the variables are null. Step through the pages to see the style follow the index feature. The expression also works in a locator frame that shows the index layer. Feature IDs are local to each layer, so use the ID comparison on the index layer. Outside a series page the comparison never matches, and every feature gets the gray style.

Reports¶

Publish › New Report lists the selected layer's records page after page; the Report pane previews it and sets it up.

  • Data: pick the detail Fields (or Autofill Fields), Group records by up to four fields (each group gets a heading and its own statistics), add Statistics (count, sum, mean, minimum, maximum), a Dynamic Picture per record (a field holding a PNG/JPEG path relative to the saved project's folder, or base64 image data), Attachments (a field listing each record's files, separated by ;; image files show when they're inside the project's folder), and a Chart Frame (records, or a field's sum, per category).
  • Report Section: Supplemental Pages of text before or after the records, New Template and Existing Template (a page layout drawn as a report page), a Related Report appended after this one, and Subreports (each record's related records through a relationship, listed beneath it).
  • A where clause or the layer's selection limits the records; a sort field orders them within their groups.
  • Publish › Report: Export Report writes a PDF, kept as vectors as a layout's is (or the first page as PNG); Save As Report File keeps the report, and Report Template its design without the data source, to start reports on other layers (Publish › Import Report).

Presentations and notebooks¶

Publish › New Presentation starts a presentation with a title page; the Presentation pane previews, edits and plays it.

  • Page: a Map page shows the map view as it is now, a Blank page shows text on a coloured page, an Image page a picture with a caption, and a Video page a video file or URL with an optional poster picture.
  • Each page has a title, speaker notes and a duration (for Play), and can move up or down.
  • Publish › Output › Presentation › Export Presentation writes an HTML slideshow (arrow keys, N for notes, P to play; videos play in place), a PDF (kept as vectors as a layout's is), or the first page as PNG. Video sources remain file paths or URLs, so keep them accessible beside the exported slideshow. Save As Presentation File in the same menu keeps it to import into other projects (.rgispres).

Analyze › New Notebook starts a notebook of text and command cells; Add and Open opens a RustGIS (.rgisnb) or Jupyter (.ipynb) notebook. A command cell runs one command per line (id {json} or id={json}, as in scripts) and shows each command's result beneath it. Notebooks save in either format. The Notebook pane opens beside the map, on half of the room right of the left pane (at least 320 points where the window allows); drag its edge to give the notebook or the map more room. The cells of a RustGIS notebook contain RustGIS commands, not Python, even in an .ipynb file. A Jupyter notebook in another language, such as Python, or one that names no language, opens with its cells and outputs (text, results, errors and PNG images) but its code cells don't run here; saving it as .ipynb keeps its kernel, metadata, cell ids and outputs, so it still opens and runs in Jupyter. RustGIS marks the .ipynb files it saves with the rustgis language, so their command cells run when opened again. A notebook holds up to 1,000 cells of up to 200,000 characters each; a larger one is refused rather than opened in part. Notebooks opened from a file or an existing project cannot run commands that read or write files or run other scripts.
